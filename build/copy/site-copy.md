### 3.4 Research threads

**Heading:** Research threads

**Intro:** Six lines of inquiry run through the work. Each one opens the projects filed under it.

**Rows (thread name, then one sentence):**

1. **Territories and Urban Systems**
   Design research at the scale of the region: mapping, scenarios and infrastructures for decarbonized and climate-adaptive futures, from the Great Lakes Megaregion to the Tennessee Valley.

2. **Responsive Envelopes and Material Systems**
   Full-scale prototypes of building skins that sense, move and respond to air, light, heat and sound, including Stratus, Resonant Chamber, Pneusystems and Infundibuliforms.

3. **Buildings and Prototypes**
   Building designs, competitions and full-scale building prototypes, from North House at the 2009 Solar Decathlon to visitor facilities at Notion, Turkey.

5. **Field Research**
   Fieldwork-based survey, site analysis and planning, led since 2014 at the ancient Ionian city of Notion with archaeologists from the University of Michigan's Kelsey Museum.

### 8.1 Kathy Velikov, FAIA

**Name (heading):** Kathy Velikov, FAIA

**Role string (one line):** Director, RVTR. Professor of Architecture and Associate Dean for Research and Creative Practice, Taubman College

**Short line (Home and cards):** Professor of Architecture and Associate Dean for Research and Creative Practice at Taubman College. Elevated to the AIA College of Fellows in 2025.

**Bio:**

<!-- KV-BIO-START -->
Kathy Velikov is an architect, researcher and educator, and a director of RVTR, the research lab she co-founded in Toronto in 2007 with Geoffrey Thün, Colin Ripley and Paul Raff. She is Professor of Architecture and Associate Dean for Research and Creative Practice at Taubman College of Architecture and Urban Planning, University of Michigan, and directs the Institute for Advanced Construction Futures, a University of Michigan Impact Institute funded in January 2026 to advance low-carbon construction. She is licensed in Michigan and Ontario. In 2025 she was elevated to the College of Fellows of the American Institute of Architects.

Her work is practice-based design research, in which new knowledge comes from design propositions and prototypes, developed through speculation, prototyping and visualization. At the material scale she prototypes building skins that integrate environmental sensing, kinetic action and responsiveness. At the territorial scale she uses urban analysis to build scenarios for decarbonized futures and new social infrastructures. With RVTR she was architect of record for North House, the solar-powered prototype for northern climates built for the 2009 U.S. Department of Energy Solar Decathlon; co-led Resonant Chamber, a rigid-origami acoustic envelope that received a U.S. patent in 2016; and co-authored Infra Eco Logi Urbanism: A Project for the Great Lakes Megaregion (Park Books, 2015). RVTR was part of the EXTRACTION team at the Canadian Pavilion, 2016 Venice Architecture Biennale. Her research has been funded by NSF, the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation, the U.S. Department of Transportation, SSHRC, Ford and Guardian Industries. She has published two books, more than twenty book chapters, thirty journal articles and forty conference papers.

She was President of ACADIA, the Association for Computer Aided Design in Architecture, from 2018 to 2020 and Vice President from 2021 to 2022. As President she created the ACADIA Diversity Committee and updated its publication ethics. She was Technical Co-Chair of ACADIA 2016, Posthuman Frontiers, hosted at the University of Michigan, and co-edited its proceedings. Since 2022 she has been External Examiner for the MArch Architectural Design (B-Pro) program at The Bartlett, UCL. She was Visiting Guest Professor at California College of the Arts in 2013 and Visiting Professor at Ryerson University in 2010, and is an invited guest critic at schools including Harvard GSD, Princeton, MIT and The Bartlett; in 2016 she spoke at the Realities and Realms Symposium at Harvard GSD. She co-edited Ambiguous Territory: Architecture, Landscape, and the Postnatural (Actar, 2022) and co-curated its exhibition, shown at four galleries from 2017 to 2019.

At Taubman College she teaches thesis and design studios and seminars in ecology and technology. She has taught more than 800 architecture students and employed more than 60 graduate research assistants. Her housing studio received the 2020 AIA/ACSA Housing Design Education Award, and in 2023 ACADIA gave her its Society Award for Leadership. She holds a B.ES and a B.Arch from the University of Waterloo and an MA in History of Art and Architecture from the University of Toronto.

### 8.2 Geoffrey Thün

**Name (heading):** Geoffrey Thün

**Role string (one line):** Director of Design Research, RVTR. Professor of Architecture, Taubman College; Associate Vice President for Research, University of Michigan

**Bio:**

<!-- GT-BIO-START -->
Geoffrey Thün is a designer, researcher and educator, and a director of RVTR, which he co-founded in Toronto in 2007 with Kathy Velikov, Colin Ripley and Paul Raff; he leads the lab's design research. He is Professor of Architecture at Taubman College of Architecture and Urban Planning and Associate Vice President for Research: Social Sciences, Humanities, and the Arts at the University of Michigan, where he is the research office's liaison to those units. He is also co-director of the university's Urban Collaboratory.

His research applies complex-systems approaches across scales: regional territories and city planning, high-performance buildings, and full-scale prototypes of responsive, kinetic building envelopes. He was faculty lead and principal investigator for North House, the Team North entry to the 2009 U.S. Department of Energy Solar Decathlon, and co-led The Stratus Project and Resonant Chamber, two prototypes of responsive interior envelopes. With Kathy Velikov and Colin Ripley he developed Conduit Urbanism and Infra Eco Logi Urbanism, design research on the Great Lakes Megaregion published by Park Books in 2015 and exhibited in Montreal, Toronto, New Haven, Ann Arbor and Knoxville. He was principal investigator for M-NEX, a Detroit project funded in 2018 through the Belmont Forum's SUGI Food-Water-Energy Nexus program, and is co-principal investigator for the University of Michigan component of NextHouse, the Purdue-led factory-built housing initiative that received a National Science Foundation Engines Development Award in 2024. In 2024 he gave the keynote "Responsive Envelopes: Paradigms and Potentials" at the Facade Tectonics World Congress.

At Taubman College he was Associate Dean for Research and Creative Practice from 2014 to 2020 and Senior Associate Dean from 2020 to 2021. He co-organized ACADIA 2016, Posthuman Frontiers, at the University of Michigan, co-edited its proceedings and catalog, and co-designed its exhibition with Matias del Campo. With Dan McTavish he planned and designed the display for New Brutalist Image 1949–55 at Tate Britain (2014–2015). He taught at the University of Waterloo from 2006 to 2009, was Adjunct Associate Professor at Ryerson University from 2011 to 2015, and has held visiting positions at California College of the Arts, Université du Québec à Montréal, Dalhousie University, Keio University and the International Solar University in Switzerland. He teaches design studios and courses on urban systems, site operations and material systems.

He shared in RVTR's recognition, including the 2009 Professional Prix de Rome in Architecture, the 2010 Architect R+D Award of Excellence and the 2011 RAIC Award of Excellence for Innovation, and received the University of Waterloo Outstanding Faculty Researcher Award in 2008. Before RVTR he worked at Dereck Revington Studio and at Baird Sampson Neuert Architects, where he was central to the French River Visitor Centre. He holds a B.A. in Sociology from the University of Western Ontario, a B.ES and a B.Arch from the University of Waterloo, and a Master of Urban Design from the University of Toronto.
